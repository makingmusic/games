# memory44 research — base-game scenario list

Source verification: the scenario list, dates, card counts, first player, and medal
targets below come from the printed rules-and-scenarios booklet of the reference game
(English 2018 printing and Dutch 2004 printing, cross-checked). Unit force mixes come
from a fan strategy database that transcribes each official setup; where a force mix was
not transcribed in text anywhere, that is noted explicitly rather than guessed.

The base game ships **16 standard scenarios** plus **1 bonus large-format scenario**
(a two-board "Overlord"-style version of scenario 5 that requires a free supplemental
rules sheet and a second copy of the game). All 16 are set on the Western Front,
06 June 1944 through late 1944, roughly in chronological order.

Conventions used below:
- "Cards" = starting Command-card hand size (Axis / Allies).
- "Medals" = victory-medal target for **both** sides unless stated otherwise.
- Temporary medal objective = the medal counts only while the capturing side occupies
  the hex; it returns to play if the unit leaves or is destroyed.
- Permanent medal objective = once captured, the medal is kept even if the unit leaves
  or is destroyed.

## Scenario-by-scenario notes

### 1. Pegasus Bridge — 06 June 1944
- Forces: Allies 9 infantry; Axis 6 infantry. No armor or artillery.
- Medals: 4. Each of the two bridges is a temporary medal objective for the Allies.
- The introductory scenario: a pre-dawn glider coup-de-main against two river/canal
  crossings. The Axis player starts with only 2 cards and draws up to 4 over the first
  two turns, modeling complete surprise. Historically elite troops fight as regular
  infantry to keep the teaching game simple. Reported win rate strongly favors Allies
  (~69% in published battle reports).

### 2. Sainte-Mère-Église — 06 June 1944
- Forces: Allies 6 infantry on the map plus up to 4 more parachute infantry units from
  the airdrop rule (6-10 total, depending on drop success); Axis 9 infantry, 1 armor.
- Medals: 4. No terrain objectives.
- Airborne night-drop flavor: before the Allied player's first turn, four infantry
  figures are dropped from a fist above the board; a figure landing off-board or on an
  occupied hex is lost (no medal to the Axis), while a figure landing on an empty hex
  is built up to a full unit. The town sits in the center with forests on both flanks.

### 3. Sword Beach — 06 June 1944
- Forces: Allies 9 infantry (some are Commando special forces) and 3 armor;
  Axis 6 infantry, 1 armor, 1 artillery.
- Medals: 5. Each town hex inland is a temporary medal objective for the Allies.
- Amphibious assault on a flat beach (no bluffs), with Commandos able to move 2 hexes
  and still battle. Only the Axis may claim bunkers as defensive positions.

### 4. Pointe-du-Hoc — 06 June 1944
- Forces: Allies 9 infantry (all Ranger special forces); Axis 5 infantry, 1 artillery.
- Medals: 4. Capturing the forest on the Axis side of the board grants the Allies a
  permanent medal (kept even if the capturing unit later leaves or dies).
- Cliff assault: the hills bordering the beach are treated as cliffs/sea bluffs —
  climbing them from the beach costs a full 2-hex move (armor cannot climb from the
  beach at all), while from inland they behave as ordinary hills. Only the Axis may
  use the bunkers.

### 5. Omaha Beach (First Assault Wave) — 06 June 1944
- Forces: Allies 10 infantry (one Ranger special-forces unit) and 3 armor;
  Axis 7 infantry and 2 artillery.
- Medals: 6. Each of three town hexes at the inland edge is a temporary medal
  objective for the Allies.
- The hardest beach: hedgehogs and wire on the sand, bluffs behind it (same cliff rule
  as Pointe-du-Hoc), Axis bunkers, and a permanent "sea wall" (two sandbag markers that
  are never removed and protect only the Allies). The Axis moves first and out-cards
  the Allies 5 to 4. Beach-side obstacles use both players' obstacle pieces.

### 6. Mont Mouchet — 10 June 1944
- Forces: Allies 7 Resistance infantry (3 figures each); Axis 6 infantry and 2 armor.
- Medals: 4. Each eliminated Axis elite armor unit counts as **two** medals for the
  Allies.
- Three German columns converging on a Resistance mountaintop redoubt in forested,
  hilly country. All Allied units are Resistance (3 figures, may enter terrain and
  still battle, may retreat 1-3 hexes per flag). Axis armor units are elite (4 tanks
  each), making them both powerful and lucrative targets. Win rate slightly favors
  Axis (~42% Allied wins reported).

### 7. Vassieux, Vercors — 24 July 1944
- Forces: Allies 10 Resistance infantry (3 figures each); Axis 6 infantry.
- Medals: 4. Special clock: if the card deck runs out before the Allies win, the Axis
  player wins the battle.
- German gliderborne surprise assault on a Resistance-held plateau airfield. The
  airfield sits center-board ringed by hills, forests and town hexes. All Allied units
  are Resistance. Historically the glider assault began 21 July 1944; the booklet
  prints 24 July as the scenario date. Reported win rate strongly favors Axis (~34%
  Allied wins) — the deck-exhaustion rule forces the Resistance to attack, not hide.

### 8. Operation Cobra — 25-26 July 1944
- Forces: Allies 10 infantry and 4 armor; Axis 7 infantry and 2 armor (one elite,
  4 tanks).
- Medals: 5. The towns of Marigny and St-Gilles are temporary medal objectives for
  the Allies.
- The American breakout from the hedgerow country: dense hedgerows channel the Allied
  infantry-and-armor push down the center and right, against a thinner Axis screen.

### 9. Operation Lüttich (Mortain counterattack) — 07 August 1944
- Forces: Allies 9 infantry, 2 armor, 2 artillery; Axis 7 infantry and 6 armor.
- Medals: 4. The town of St-Hilaire is a temporary medal objective for the **Axis**.
- Night-launched armored counteroffensive. Axis special forces are elite grenadiers
  (move 2 and battle) and elite armor (4 tanks). Climbing a hill from low ground costs
  a 2-hex move. While an Allied unit holds Hill 317, the Allied player may play Recon
  cards as air-strike cards in that section (roll 2 dice per targeted hex).

### 10. Toulon — 20-26 August 1944
- Forces: Allies 10 infantry (one Commando and one Ranger special-forces unit) and
  4 armor; Axis 10 infantry and 1 artillery.
- Medals: 6, **or** the Allies win immediately if they occupy all 3 hexes of Toulon at
  the end of a turn.
- French assault on the port city: angled bands of hilly forest split the flanks from
  the center, so early unit placement matters. Win rate favors Allies (~66%).

### 11. Liberation of Paris — 24 August 1944
- Forces: Allies 6 infantry and 8 armor; Axis 4 infantry, 2 armor, 2 artillery.
- Medals: 5. Each "outskirts of Paris" town hex on the Axis side is a **permanent**
  medal objective for the Allies.
- An armor-heavy Allied dash through forested countryside toward the city edge,
  against a thin but sandbagged Axis screen. The permanent-objective rule means the
  Allies can raid, score, and move on.

### 12. Montélimar — 25 August 1944
- Forces: Allies 9 infantry, 2 armor, 1 artillery; Axis 9 infantry (regular Axis
  infantry here have only 3 figures), 4 armor, 1 artillery.
- Medals: 6. The towns of Bonlieu and Grane are temporary medal objectives for the
  Axis; one temporary medal for the Allies if any of their units occupies a hex
  adjacent to the Rhône river (one medal only, no matter how many hexes are held).
- Meeting engagement along a river-bounded valley: a hill cluster dominates the center,
  hills north of the Condillac pass are impassable, and both sides hunt objective
  medals in opposite directions. Axis elite grenadiers move 2 and battle.

### 13. Arnhem Bridge — 17-19 September 1944
- Forces: unit counts were not transcribed in text sources (setup is shown graphically).
  Composition: the Allies are all elite airborne infantry plus artillery, dug in behind
  sandbags around the bridge's north end; the Axis have the larger force — elite
  grenadiers and 4-tank elite armor — but much of it starts trapped behind the wide
  Rhine on the far side.
- Medals: 5. No terrain objectives; pure attrition race.
- Units may enter or leave the bridge only via its two marked end hexes; movement along
  the span is normal, and infantry may not fire across the Rhine (too wide). The Axis
  must force the bridge crossing into a defensive kill zone. Reported win rate heavily
  favors Allies (~81%).

### 14. Arracourt — 19 September 1944
- Forces: unit counts were not transcribed in text sources. Composition: an
  armor-on-armor battle — both sides field large tank forces (Axis panzer brigades
  counterattacking, US armor defending around a five-hex ridge that crosses one flank
  into the center, with a smaller ridge in front of it).
- Medals: 6. No terrain objectives or special rules — a straight-up tank duel defined
  by the ridges, hedgerows and morning fog of the historical fight. Win rate favors
  Allies (~60%).

### 15. St. Vith, Ardennes — 20-22 December 1944
- Forces: Allies 7 infantry and 6 armor; Axis 10 infantry, 4 armor, 1 artillery.
- Medals: 6. No terrain objectives.
- Bulge assault on a road-hub town: the Allies defend a bowl ringed by forests with an
  impassable ridge (the Prümerberg) along their back edge — the center is a kill zone.
  All Axis armor is elite (4 tanks each). The Axis out-numbers and out-cards the Allies
  (5 vs 4) and moves first.

### 16. Saverne Gap, Vosges — 19-21 November 1944 (see note)
- Forces: unit counts were not transcribed in text sources. Composition: Allies press
  the pass with infantry plus a strong armor wing on one flank; the Axis are infantry
  dug into forests and the town of Saverne itself.
- Medals: 5, **or** the Allies win immediately if they occupy 2 of the 3 Saverne town
  hexes at the end of their turn.
- A pincer around a mountain pass: hills are impassable and artillery may not fire over
  them, effectively splitting the board into three separate battles. Reported win rate
  slightly favors Axis (~46% Allied wins).
- Date note: the printed scenario header and background paragraph say "September 19-21,
  1944", but the historical Saverne Gap action (XV Corps at Phalsbourg, Leclerc's
  armored division pincers, road to Strasbourg) took place in **November** 1944 —
  Strasbourg fell on 23 November 1944. Treat the booklet's "September" as a printing
  error.

### Bonus: Omaha Beach — Overlord version — 06 June 1944
- Same battle as scenario 5 played across two joined beach boards (a multi-board
  format supporting team play up to 8 players), requiring the free supplemental
  large-format rules. 8 cards per side, Axis moves first, 8 medals to win. Town hexes
  are temporary medal objectives for the Allies, and Allied units that exit the board
  along the Axis edge score a permanent medal each (breakthrough/exfiltration rule).
  Either side may also play Recon cards as a 4-dice barrage against a single target.

## Cross-cutting observations (from the confirmed data)

- Medal targets: 4 (×5 scenarios), 5 (×5), 6 (×5), plus one 8 for the bonus big-board
  game. Medals usually equal roughly half the defender's unit count.
- Card hands: range from the Pegasus Bridge 2-vs-6 shock asymmetry up to 6-vs-6;
  the attacker usually (not always) holds more cards and moves first. Omaha and
  St. Vith invert this — the defender is strong, so the attacker moves first **and**
  holds more cards while the defender sits on better terrain.
- 5 of 16 scenarios are beach/amphibious or bridge-crossing assaults; 3 feature
  Resistance forces; 2 are armor-dominated meeting engagements (Montélimar, Arracourt).
- Objective medals come in three functional flavors: temporary hold (most towns,
  bridges), permanent capture (Paris outskirts, Pointe-du-Hoc forest), and special
  triggers (double-value elite armor at Mont Mouchet; deck-exhaustion timer at
  Vassieux; exit-the-board medals in the bonus scenario; instant-win occupation
  conditions at Toulon and Saverne).
