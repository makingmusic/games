# memory44 — Visual Style (Functional Description)

A functional description of the game's visual presentation, written so an artist can produce
original work in a comparable-but-distinct style. No images are included or referenced.
This describes what each component class communicates visually and how, not any specific artwork.

## Overall Art Direction

The whole product aims for the feel of a mid-20th-century illustrated history book crossed with
a toy-soldier playset: warm, hand-painted gouache/oil-style illustration rather than photography
or digital realism; muted, slightly desaturated period colors (olive drab, khaki, field grey,
dusty sand, faded khaki-green); and consistently top-down or slightly elevated viewpoints on all
gameplay surfaces so pieces read clearly from a seated player's position. Graphic design is clean
and functional — period-evocative stencil-ish typography and military-map conventions (dotted
section lines, star insignia) — but never cluttered: the information layer always wins over
decoration.

## Game Board

- Top-down painted landscape, hexagonal grid overlaid in a thin, unobtrusive line.
- **Countryside side:** rolling green farmland — varied greens broken by lighter meadows and
  faint dirt paths; deliberately empty (terrain tiles supply the detail).
- **Beach side:** horizontal bands from one long edge — deep blue-green sea with pale wave
  foam lines, a wide tan sand strip, then scrubby green-brown vegetation inland.
- Two red/brown dotted lines run across the grid dividing it into left flank, center, and right
  flank sections; the lines are the only strong graphic element on the board.
- Style note: the board is a quiet backdrop; saturation is kept low so colored plastic pieces pop.

## Miniatures

- Soft, slightly flexible plastic; infantry roughly 20 mm (near 1:72) scale; vehicles and guns
  scaled to fit a hex alongside 3–4 pieces.
- **One identical pose per nation per type** — every Allied infantryman is the same advancing
  rifleman sculpt, every Axis infantryman the same standing/firing sculpt. Recognition is by
  silhouette and color, not pose variety.
- Sculpts are simplified but anatomically believable: period helmets, webbing, and weapons are
  suggested rather than crisply detailed; faces are smooth. Tanks are recognizable period
  silhouettes (sloped-armor medium tank for the Axis, rounded-hull medium tank for the Allies)
  with molded tracks and turrets. Artillery pieces are a field gun with two crew figures molded
  onto a shared base, counting as a single piece.
- Unpainted single-color castings: **olive green (Allies)** vs **blue-grey (Axis)**; expansions
  add brown/tan/khaki tones for additional nations. The toy-soldier monochrome look is a
  deliberate, charming part of the identity — many owners hand-paint them.

## Command Cards

Standard card-game size with a consistent anatomy:

- **Title banner** across the top naming the order.
- **Central painted vignette**: a small period-flavored illustration (soldiers advancing, a
  tank charge, a medic scene) in the same painterly style as the box art.
- **Effect text** in a lower panel, written as terse orders.
- **Color coding is the primary functional signal**: Section cards have a **green** background
  and carry a small map-diagram strip showing which of the three board sections (left/center/
  right) is activated, plus a large numeral or "ALL" showing how many units may be ordered.
  Tactic cards have a **grey/khaki** background and no section diagram, since their effects are
  special actions rather than section orders.
- Card backs share a single camouflage/insignia design so the two types are indistinguishable
  in hand from the back.

## Battle Dice

Wooden cubes with **engraved, inked symbols instead of pips** — a key tactile identity element.
The six faces: a stylized infantryman silhouette (two faces), a tank silhouette, a grenade, a
star, and a flag. Symbols are simple, chunky one-color pictograms readable at arm's length;
they double as the game's icon language (the same silhouettes appear on cards and summary
sheets). The revised edition switched to molded plastic dice with brightly colored symbols but
kept identical iconography.

## Terrain Hex Tiles

- Punchboard hexes in thick cardstock, illustrated **top-down in the same painterly style** as
  the board, sized to sit flush over board hexes.
- Each terrain family has an instantly readable visual shorthand: forests = clusters of dark
  green treetops; hills = tan/brown contour shading with a crest highlight; towns = red-roofed
  building clusters seen from above; hedgerows = thick dark-green linear borders tracing field
  edges; rivers = blue ribbons in straight or curved segments.
- Double-sided pairings deliberately combine different families, so the same physical tile
  stock serves many scenarios.
- Obstacle tokens (sandbags, wire, hedgehogs, bunkers) are small rectangular punchboard pieces
  using the same top-down miniature-diorama look — sandbag rings, coiled wire, crossed steel
  beams — drawn with strong silhouettes so they read over busy tile art.

## Tokens, Badges, and Medals

- Round **victory medal tokens** styled as military decorations: a star medallion, one face per
  side (Allied star vs Axis cross-style emblem), in gold/red period tones.
- **Special-unit badges** are small square tokens bearing simple national/elite-unit insignia,
  placed beside a unit's figures to mark elite status.
- **Summary cards** share the command-card painterly frame but are information-dense: a terrain
  illustration thumbnail plus a compact table of movement and combat effects.

## Rules & Scenario Booklets

- Full color throughout, laid out like an illustrated field manual: generous painted spot
  illustrations, diagrammed examples with the actual board grid, and captions.
- Each scenario page is a **top-down color map of the board** with terrain tiles and unit
  icons drawn in place, flanked by historical background text, special rules, and medal
  objectives — the setup diagram is the visual centerpiece and is functional first.

## Box Art Composition

- A painted, cinematic montage rather than a photograph: foreground infantry in action poses
  (charging, firing) rendered largest, with tanks, landing craft, and aircraft receding into a
  smoke-and-sky background that suggests a beach landing at dawn.
- Warm amber/olive palette with cool sky contrast; the game title in a stenciled period-style
  logotype with a star motif; composition is diagonal and energetic, evoking 1940s war-poster
  and pulp-illustration traditions without copying any specific poster.
- Back cover: photographs of the actual components in play — the box sells the toy-soldier
  tactility as much as the artwork.

## Guidance for an Original Comparable Style

To sit in the same family without imitation: keep (1) top-down painterly terrain with low-saturation
boards and high-readability tiles, (2) monochrome toy-soldier plastics with one pose per type and
color-coded armies, (3) symbol-driven custom dice whose icons recur across all components, and
(4) function-first card anatomy with background color as the card-type signal. Differentiate via a
distinct setting palette, an original insignia/icon system (avoid star-and-cross medal likenesses),
original sculpt poses and vehicle silhouettes, and original card frames and typography.
